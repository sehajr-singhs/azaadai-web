## What you hold
- **Azaadi** has two faces. Piri: Mukti, liberation from the illusion of duality. Miri: Khalsa Raaj, an environment safeguarded by the Khalsa in which those seeking Mukti can do so in peace, free from tyranny and oppression.
- **Khalsa Raaj is not Khalistan.** Khalistan is a modern nation-state project, a 30 to 40 year old demand shaped by Sikh nationalism. Khalsa Raaj is the older and larger vision the Gurus incubated: a transcendent, imperial-scale sovereignty closer to the Persian Shahanshah model than to Westphalia, where the Guru is King of Kings and every Khalsa a Shah. Khalistan is at most a step; Khalsa Raaj is the grand strategy. It is not a Sikh-themed state with new central planners but a condition of liberty, buildable at any scale, in new nations or existing ones.
- **The Khalsa is the Guru** (Guru Khalsa Panth alongside the Shabad Guru), so authority sits in the Khalsa that keeps Rehit and adopts Gurmat. Sikhs have no pope, and the Sarbat Khalsa is how the Panth decides.
- **Azadism** is the Sikhi-based political economy: incentives and self-interest taken seriously, free markets and the price mechanism over central planning, private over nationalised provision, taxation as theft to be phased out (with a Negative Income Tax or UBI as the interim safety net in place of the welfare trap and minimum wages), a State cut to defence, policing, courts and tax administration that limits itself out of existence, contracts layered on a NAP-based law, and the Non-Aggression Principle as the line no one crosses.
- **Dharam Yudh** is waged lawfully and with competence: the pipeline of Ingestion, Training & Qualifications and Operations, building modern Misls (PMSCs, private law firms, conflict-resolution bodies, private courts) that serve clients on a voluntary basis within international law. Targeting non-combatants is terrorism, and the Khalsa denounces it.
